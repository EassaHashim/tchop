Common objections by prospect segment, with recommended responses. Use this file to inform cold email, sales enablement, copywriting, and email sequences.

## Universal objections (all segments)

### "We already have a tool for this."
**Usually means:** Microsoft Teams, SharePoint, Confluence, an intranet, or a mix of fragmented tools.

**Response:**
Most organizations do — but those tools were built for information transfer, not engagement. tchop combines communication and community on a platform that feels like a modern social app — the UX employees actually want to use. SharePoint and Teams push content out. tchop gets people to come back, participate, and stay connected. It sits alongside your existing tools and integrates with SSO on day one.

---

**Response:**
Understood. For context, most organizations that come to us are spending more on fragmented tools — multiple SaaS licences, agency fees for employee comms, or IT support for legacy systems — than tchop's combined setup and licence fee. We're happy to share a cost comparison based on your current setup if that's useful.

---

### "We'd need IT/procurement involved."
**Response:**
That's normal, and we're set up for it. tchop is GDPR-compliant, supports SSO, and can run on EU-sovereign hosting. We have security documentation ready and regularly go through procurement processes with enterprise clients like AOK. Happy to send materials directly, or set up a call that includes your IT or procurement contact.

## ICP 1 — Enterprise internal comms

### "Our employees are already on Teams/Slack."
**Response:**
Teams and Slack work well for desk workers. tchop is designed for the workers who aren't in those tools — warehouse staff, nurses, store employees, drivers. The average manufacturing or logistics company has 60–70% non-desk workers who are unreachable via Teams. tchop fills that gap without replacing what's already working.

---

### "We don't want another app. App fatigue is real."
**Response:**
That's a legitimate concern. tchop replaces fragmented channels — not adds to them. Most clients retire 2–4 tools when they launch. Because it's white-labeled, employees see it as the company app, not another vendor tool. Adoption rates for clients like AOK have been strong precisely because it's the single place for everything.

---

### "We're not sure employees will actually use it."
**Response:**
That's the right question to ask. tchop is built for low-friction adoption — no corporate email required, works on personal devices, one-tap access. We've seen consistently higher adoption than legacy intranet tools. We're happy to share adoption benchmarks from comparable organizations.

---

### "We're evaluating Staffbase."
**Response:**
Staffbase is a strong product — we respect them. The main differences: tchop is faster to set up (days vs months), has a lower total cost, and is better suited for comms teams that want to do more with less — AI-powered automation means a small comms team can manage a platform at enterprise scale without constant manual effort. Staffbase requires significant ongoing resource to get full value from. If your comms team is lean relative to your employee headcount, tchop is likely a better fit.

---

### "We have no way to measure whether our comms are actually working."
**Response:**
That's a core problem tchop solves. The platform gives you read receipts, engagement rates, push notification open rates, and channel-level analytics — so you can see exactly which content reaches which employees and what drives action. That feedback loop is something most intranet and email-based tools can't provide.

## ICP 2 — Brand community

### "We're building our community on [Circle / Slack / Discord / Facebook Groups]."
**Response:**
Those platforms own your audience. Every community built on Circle, Slack, or Facebook is one policy change away from losing access to its members. tchop gives you a fully white-labeled, owned community — your brand, your data, no third-party dependency. For brands investing in long-term community, that's not a small distinction.

---

### "Our community is small — we're not ready for a platform yet."
**Response:**
Most clients launch when they have a few hundred active members — not thousands. The earlier you own your community infrastructure, the easier migration becomes later. Starting on a rented platform and migrating later is expensive and causes churn. Setup takes 1–2 weeks and pricing scales with your community size.

---

### "We already have an app."
**Response:**
Is it white-labeled and community-enabled? Many brand apps are content or e-commerce focused without the community and engagement layer. tchop adds community features, push notifications, content channels, and direct member communication on top of what you likely have — or can replace it entirely if the build/maintenance cost is a pain point.

---

### "We get enough data from social media analytics."
**Response:**
Social analytics tells you how your content performs on someone else's platform — not who your audience actually is. tchop gives you first-party data: real user profiles, behavior inside your community, and direct relationships you own. When Meta changes its algorithm or restricts data access, that insight disappears. First-party data doesn't.

## ICP 4 + 5 — News / Media / Publishers

### "We can build an app with our CMS."
**Response:**
Some CMS platforms offer app modules — but they're designed for content distribution, not community. tchop gives you push notifications, direct messaging, community features, and subscriber management in one product, without the custom development overhead. Funke Digital uses tchop to extend their existing CMS, not replace it.

---

### "We don't have the technical resources to run this."
**Response:**
That's exactly what tchop is designed for. The dashboard is no-code — content editors manage the app without involving IT. We handle infrastructure, updates, and compliance. Most media clients are fully self-sufficient within two weeks of launch.

---

### "Our audience is on social media, not an app."
**Response:**
That's true now — but dependence on social platforms for audience reach is increasingly fragile. Algorithm changes, declining organic reach, and rising ad costs are pushing media companies toward owned channels. tchop is the infrastructure for that owned audience. San Angelo LIVE grew their direct subscriber base after moving away from Facebook-first distribution.

---

### "Our traffic is declining but we don't know how to fix it."
**Response:**
Declining traffic from Google and social is a structural shift, not a temporary dip. tchop gives you a direct channel to your audience that's algorithm-independent — push notifications, in-app content, and community features that bring readers back without relying on external platforms to surface your content.

---

### "We want to personalize content but don't have the data."
**Response:**
tchop builds that data over time. You see which readers engage with which topics, how long they spend, what they click — and can use that to segment push notifications and content channels accordingly. It's the user data layer most publisher CMSes don't provide.

---

### "We're a small local publisher — this feels too big for us." (ICP 5)
**Response:**
tchop has a specific offering for local and hyperlocal publishers — lower setup cost, flat monthly pricing, and a no-code dashboard that a one- or two-person team can manage. San Angelo LIVE runs their community app without a dedicated tech person. The platform is designed to help small newsrooms punch above their weight.

## Agency objections (ICP 3)

### "We're a services business, not a software business."
**Response:**
That's the point. tchop handles the software — you wrap your strategy, content, and change management services around it. You're not reselling SaaS, you're offering a more complete service. Agencies that partner with tchop retain clients longer because the platform creates ongoing dependency on their expertise.

---

### "It's hard to show clients ROI on comms work."
**Response:**
That's exactly what a platform like tchop solves. When your client's employees or community members are inside a tchop-powered app, you get engagement data — open rates, read rates, content performance, user growth. That gives you the numbers to show the value of your strategic and content work. Comms campaigns become measurable for the first time.

---

### "Our clients will want to buy direct."
**Response:**
Some will. But clients who are introduced through an agency typically stay in that relationship — especially when the agency provides onboarding, training, and content support. We structure partnerships so the agency retains the relationship and the margin. Happy to share how current agency partners work with us.
