Before we dive in

Some learnings upfront from many internal comms projects

  • Don't get lost in small features or functional details - this is often distracting in the early stages
  • Focus on content, value for your employees and the operations that enable this first
  • Understand what you can reuse or simply integrate - and what you will need to create or recreate
  • How can you ensure a great mix that provides maximum value?
  • Start with a simple structure and then grow - don't build too much complexity in the beginning

Understanding tchop

Content Hierarchy

Four layers make up every tchop app. Think of channels as audiences, mixes as sections, cards as individual posts.

O
Organisation
Top level. Your entire app instance. Holds all content, users, settings. One organisation = one branded app.
C
Channels
Who sees what. Separate spaces for departments, regions, topics, or target groups. Each with its own editors and content.
M
Mixes
Sections within a channel. The news feed, topic areas, pinned resources. This is where you organise your content structure.
K
Content Cards
The atomic unit. Every piece of content is a card. 12 different types for different needs. Cards live inside mixes.

Card Deep Dive

Article Card

What it does
  • Links to any external URL. The primary curation card.
  • Auto-scrapes teaser data (title, image, description).
  • Opens in-app browser (mobile) or new tab (web).
  • 4 teaser styles to keep feeds visually varied.
Best for
  • Curating news, press mentions, partner content
  • Linking to intranet, HR portals, training resources
Good to know
  • Content lives externally - if URL goes down, card breaks

Card Deep Dive

Long Post Card

What it does
  • Native longform article with a block-based editor.
  • Opens in a native reader view (not a browser).
  • 17 block types: text, images, galleries, quotes, tables, embeds, audio, video, files, and more.
Best for
  • CEO messages, strategy updates, employee spotlights
  • Onboarding guides, policy explainers, reports
Good to know
  • Can only be edited via web app, not mobile

Card Deep Dive

Text Card

What it does
  • Simple text post - like a status update.
  • No media required. Basic formatting only.
  • Shows up to 4 lines in feed with "Read more".
Best for
  • Quick announcements, reminders, shout-outs
  • User-generated updates from the field
Good to know
  • Text only - if you need media, use Image or Long Post

Card Deep Dive

Image Card

What it does
  • Single image with optional text. Image is mandatory.
  • Supports GIF. Caption and copyright per image.
Best for
  • Team photos, event moments, infographics
  • User-generated content from employees
Good to know
  • Single image, but can also have headlines or description text with flexible length. Simple formatting only - complex formatting only in Long Post.

Card Deep Dive

Gallery Card

What it does
  • 2-20 images in a swipeable carousel.
  • Each image has its own caption and copyright.
  • First image shows in feed; users swipe for the rest.
Best for
  • Event photo albums, project milestones
  • Step-by-step visual guides, office tours
Good to know
  • Minimum 2 images required. Use captions to tell a story.

Card Deep Dive

Video Card

What it does
  • Native video player with teaser image. Plays inline.
  • Teaser image is mandatory - shows with a play icon.
  • Supports any video format.
Best for
  • CEO messages, townhall recordings, training clips
  • Employee-generated video content
Good to know
  • 100 MB limit. Keep videos short (1-3 min).

Audio Card

What it does
  • Native audio player with optional teaser image.

  • Supports .mp3, .wav, .aiff formats.

Card Deep Dive

PDF Card

What it does
  • PDF viewer with auto-generated teaser from first page.
  • Opens in native viewer (mobile) or new tab (web).
  • Teaser image can be replaced with a custom one.
Best for

Card Deep Dive

Thread Card

What it does
  • Discussion starter - opens directly into comments.
  • Only requires a headline. The simplest card type.
  • Works like a forum thread for Q&A and dialogue.
Best for
  • Q&A sessions, AMAs with leadership
  • Brainstorming, idea collection, feedback
Good to know
  • Headline only - no media. Pin for visibility.

Card Deep Dive

Poll Card

What it does
  • Interactive voting directly in the feed - one tap.
  • Single or multi-choice. Results update dynamically.
  • Optional expiration date and result visibility control.
Best for
  • Pulse surveys, sentiment checks
  • Event planning, editorial input, quick decisions
Good to know
  • Cannot edit after publishing or first vote. 3-5 options ideal.

Card Deep Dive

Social Card

What it does
  • Mirrors social media posts from Facebook, Instagram, X, TikTok.
  • Auto-scrapes author, content, images from the URL.
  • Clicking opens the original social post.
Best for
  • Sharing company social posts with employees
  • Curating industry voices, employer branding
Good to know
  • Can be auto-imported via RSS or social integrations

Best Practice

When content becomes social

How it works
  • Every card can display the user who posted it - or not. This is a setting per mix.
  • Use this to separate corporate content (no user shown) from personal posts (user avatar + name visible).
Our recommendation
  • Human level is king. Most posts should show the person behind them.
  • Only selected posts should appear "from corporate" without a user displayed.
  • Even the comms team should mainly act as the persons they are.
  • Let C-level post directly - or have ghostwriters do it. Personal voices drive engagement.

Understanding tchop

Mixes: Your Content Architecture

How mixes appear in the app
  • News Feed the main screen users see first. Built from one or more mixes.
  • Navigation List browsable lists of sections. Your content menu.
  • Pinned Tab single feeds you want to give quick access. Fixed in bottom navigation.
  • Horizontal Module swipeable carousel within the feed. Highlights and features.
Example structure for SIL
  • Main Feed latest from across SIL, curated daily
  • From Leadership Executive Director updates, strategic messages
  • Team Stories people spotlights, project updates, field reports
  • Resources policies, guides, strategic plan (pinned tab)
  • Events townhalls, celebrations, key dates (horizontal carousel)
Prepare internally

Think of mixes like feeds. What kind of feeds do you want to deliver centrally, and what should regions do locally?

Understanding tchop

Channels: Who Sees What

Channel use cases
  • All Staff company-wide content visible to everyone
  • Leadership exec-only updates, board materials, strategic planning
  • Regional Africa, Asia Pacific, Americas - localised content
  • Department HR, IT, Finance, Operations - team-specific spaces
  • Project temporary channels for initiatives, working groups
Access & permissions
  • Org Admin full access to everything. Your platform owner.
  • Channel Admin manages one channel. Content, users, settings.
  • Editor creates and publishes content within a channel.
  • Limited Editor can post from the app. No dashboard access.
  • Reader consumes content, comments, reacts.
Prepare internally

Map your organisation to channels. Start simple - you can always add more later. Which audiences need separate spaces?

Developing Your Strategy

Push Notifications: Use With Care

Types available
  • Editorial Push manually crafted, linked to a specific card or URL
  • Auto-Push per Mix every new card in a mix triggers a push automatically
  • Retention Push automated re-engagement for inactive users
  • Chat Push real-time notifications for messages
Best practices
  • Be selective. Not everything deserves a push notification.
  • Reserve push for high-value content. Leadership messages, breaking news, urgent updates.
  • Avoid fatigue. More than 2-3 pushes per day and users will mute you.
  • Users control their own settings. Respect that.
Prepare internally

Decide upfront: what is push-worthy? Leadership messages? Breaking news? Everything? Write it down.

Developing Your Strategy

Multilingual: What Gets Translated?

Questions to answer internally
  • Which languages are essential at launch?
  • What content must be translated vs. English-only?
  • Who reviews translations for accuracy?
  • How fast do translations need to be published?
Approaches available
  • Separate language channels per region, curated independently
  • Mixed-language feeds with language tags for filtering
  • AI-assisted translation for speed, human review for key messages
  • Visual-first formats video, images, galleries that work across languages
Prepare internally

With 90+ nationalities, this is a key decision. Start simple - you can always expand language coverage later.

Organising Your Team

Governance: Rules of the Road

Content guidelines to define
  • Tone of voice how should SIL sound on the app? Formal, warm, inspiring?
  • Post length max words for different card types
  • Image standards quality, format, sizing, attribution requirements
  • Branding consistent use of SIL name, logos, terminology
Operational rules to agree on
  • Response times who replies to comments? How quickly?
  • Archiving when does content get removed or archived?
  • Crisis comms who publishes urgent messages? What's the escalation path?
  • User-generated content can staff post? Where? What's moderated?
Prepare internally

Write these down before launch. Even a one-page document helps. It doesn't need to be perfect - just agreed upon.

Learnings from Other Clients

What Works

1
Start with 2-3 mixes, not 20
Less is more. Expand over time as your team gets comfortable.
2
Leadership content on day one
A CEO or ED message drives first-week adoption like nothing else.
3
One poll per week
Simple interactions double engagement rates. People love to vote.
4
Pre-load content before inviting users
Nobody wants an empty app. Have 2 weeks of content ready at launch.
5
Name your app
A branded name creates ownership and identity. "The SIL App" works - a custom name works better.
6
Weekly editor sync for 90 days
Keep momentum. Share learnings. Celebrate wins. Adjust fast.

Learnings from Other Clients

Common Pitfalls to Avoid

What to avoid
  • Too many channels from the start. Complexity kills adoption.
  • No editorial rhythm. Without a cadence, the app goes silent.
  • Treating it like email. Broadcasting doesn't build community.
  • No content on launch day. First impressions matter enormously.
How others recovered
  • Consolidate channels. Focus on 2-3, archive the rest.
  • Establish one recurring format first. A weekly update is enough to start.
  • Shift from broadcasting to dialogue. Polls, threads, and Q&A change the dynamic.
  • Pre-fill with 2 weeks of content before inviting anyone to the app.
